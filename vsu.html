Virginia State University

The offices you must engage, and when.

VSU publishes real internal deadlines and a real multi-office pre-proposal process. Most missed submissions are not writing failures — they are routing failures. This page maps the whole path, marks what is verified against VSU’s own pages, and flags the handful of things you must confirm with a human before you rely on them.

Sourcing. Everything marked verified comes from a VSU page or document linked inline. 1. The working-backwards timeline

VSU’s published rule is blunt: “All grant proposals, regardless of funding entity or submission portal, must be routed for approval through Cayuse. Proposals not approved in Cayuse may be refused by the university at the time of funding.” Build every calendar backwards from the sponsor deadline.

  1. T–60 days or earlierStart planning verified

    VSU states that “the ideal time to begin planning your proposal is at least 2 months before the submission deadline.” This is also when you should run a Go/No-Go screen, contact the program officer, and settle the team. If you are reading this inside 30 days, jump straight to the FCOI and Cayuse steps and call OSRP today.

  2. T–45 to T–30 daysTalk to Research Development

    Dr. Lora Battle Bailey, Director for Research Development (lbbailey@vsu.edu, 804-524-5385) supports funding-opportunity identification, proposal development, collaborator identification, and grantsmanship training. This is the office to bring a concept to before it is a draft.

  3. T–30 calendar days — HARD GATEGrant Notice of Intent (GNoI) + the full pre-proposal planning packet verified

    VSU requires the Grant Notice of Intent Form “NO LESS THAN 30 DAYS before the submission deadline,” and the pre-proposal planning documents “no less than 30 calendar days prior.” The packet is not one form — it is one form per administrative unit:

    Pre-proposal planning checklist · Procurement planning form (Purchasing) · Technology planning form (IT) · Human resources planning form (HR) · Facilities planning form · Cost sharing form

    Once OSRP has these, a meeting is convened between you, OSRP, and every unit you flagged. This is unusually well-specified for an institution of VSU’s size and it is the mechanism by which Purchasing, IT, HR, and Facilities formally enter your proposal. Do not skip it and do not treat it as paperwork — it is where you find out that the equipment you budgeted has a twelve-week procurement lead time.

  4. Before you submit to ANY federal agency — HARD GATEFinancial Conflict of Interest disclosure verified

    Investigators must file a Financial Interests Report Form before submitting a proposal to any federal agency, annually, within 30 days of a new appointment, and within 30 days of acquiring a new significant financial interest. VSU’s language on failure is explicit: “the associated proposal submission will be delayed until compliance is achieved. This could jeopardize the timely submission of the proposal.”

    Signed original to OSRP c/o Daniel Stoelting, or scanned from a VSU address to dstoelting@vsu.edu. CITI COI training is required for PHS/NIH investigators and renews at least every four years. CITI RCR training is required for NSF, USDA, and PHS projects.

  5. T–21 daysBudget locked, subawards requested

    Subrecipient packets take longer than anyone plans for — each partner needs a scope, a budget, a budget justification, a commitment letter, and their own institutional signature. Consultants need a rate, a scope, and a letter. If human subjects are involved, your IRB submission should be moving in parallel; VSU requires IRB approval before any data collection begins, and while that is not a submission gate for most sponsors, some require it at award.

  6. T–7 days — HARD GATERoute the complete proposal in Cayuse SP verified

    “Route your completed proposal including the signed Request for Institutional Cost Sharing form, if applicable, through Cayuse’s Sponsored Projects (SP) application NO LESS THAN 7 DAYS prior to the grant submission deadline.” VSU’s tenant is at vsu.app.cayuse.com/sp/proposals. Grants.gov submissions go system-to-system through Cayuse Proposals.

    The approval chain, taken from the signature block on VSU’s own cost-sharing form: Department Chair → Dean of the College → Director, OSRP. Every one of those people has a calendar and some of them travel. Seven days is the floor, not the target.

  7. T–2 daysGold Team pass

    Internal consistency, page limits, formatting, attachments, identifiers, and the embarrassment scan. See the Gold Team prompt.

  8. T–1 business daySubmit early

    Grants.gov validation errors are not a permitted reason for late submission at NIH, and NIH “will not solicit or accept revised, corrected, or replacement materials.” Submit with a full business day of margin so a rejected validation can be fixed and resubmitted before the clock runs out.

Note on “days.” VSU’s routing page says “30 DAYS” and “7 DAYS” without qualifying them; the pre-proposal planning page says “30 calendar days,” and VSU’s research misconduct policy defines “Day means calendar day” institution-wide. The working assumption is calendar days — which is the stricter reading — but confirm the 7-day figure with pre-award before you plan against it.

Institutional data for your narrative — OPIE

The Office of Planning and Institutional Effectiveness holds Institutional Research. Published resources include Facts & Quick Charts, the Common Data Set, and SCHEV peer and aspirant institution lists.

For anything not in the published reports there is a data request form. Request early — institutional data requests are not same-day. VSU’s research statistics page also publishes grant success rate, research income and expenditures, and publication rate, which is exactly what a capacity narrative needs.

Purchasing, IT, HR, Facilities — the pre-proposal planning forms

Each of these units has its own planning form due with the T–30 packet, and OSRP convenes a meeting with the ones you flag.

Purchasing on grants: under $5,000 you can order directly through eVA if the vendor is registered, or use a purchase card with training. $5,000 and above must go through OSRP. Without a purchase card, complete an OSRP purchase request form and email it to Sharon Evans with the PI name, account number, vendor, and quotes. VSU gives preference to SWaM (Small, Women-owned, and Minority-owned) businesses and Commonwealth contract vendors — budget accordingly and build in lead time.

Why this matters at proposal stage: if your methods depend on equipment, software licensing, a new hire, or renovated space, the answer to “can we actually get this, and how fast” has to arrive before you promise it in a timeline.

Post-award — Grants Accounting and the Controller

OSRP monitors awards, including semi-annual visits to PIs. Expenditures are reviewed for allowability, allocability, reasonableness, and necessity. Time and effort reports must agree with salaries charged in Banner.

The 90-day rule: “No corrections will be made to grants after 90 days from the posting of expenditures without written approval from the Provost.” Watch your ledgers monthly, not annually.

University Controller: Stephen C. Huffman, CPA. CFO: Kevin Davenport, VP for Administration and Finance.

Title III

Title III is at 3218 Barnes Street, first floor, 804-524-5231. It handles federal and university reporting requirements, communications with the U.S. Department of Education, and grant compliance monitoring. Structurally notable: the Title III coordinator reports directly to the President, not through the Provost or DivRED. If your project touches Title III activity funds, that reporting line matters for who has to agree.

Agricultural Research Station and 1890 land-grant status

The Agricultural Research Station carries VSU’s land-grant research mission, aimed at small, part-time, and limited-resource farmers. It draws on four streams: federal congressional appropriations, USDA NIFA formula funding (the 1890 capacity mechanism), Commonwealth allocations, and competitive grants including the USDA Capacity Building Program.

VSU’s faculty handbook establishes a distinct Agricultural Research and Extension Faculty track with its own appointment and promotion policies and separate ranks. Virginia Cooperative Extension at VSU runs its own site at ext.vsu.edu.

confirm The ARS Director is not named publicly, Evans-Allen is not named on VSU’s ARS pages even though the formula funding described is that mechanism, and whether 1890 capacity projects also route through NIFA’s REEport in addition to Cayuse is unclear. Ask the College of Agriculture.

4. Compliance gates that can stop a submission

IRB — human subjects

irb@vsu.edu. OSRP liaison: Sharon Evans. FWA00027945. Complies with 45 CFR 46 and HIPAA. Submission runs through an online Microsoft Forms application with video and text walkthroughs. Templates exist for consent, assent, continuing review, amendment, closure, and adverse events.

“All research involving human subjects must be reviewed and approved by the IRB before any data collection begins.” confirm The IRB chair is not named publicly, review turnaround times are not published, and — although CITI is explicitly required for RCR and COI — the human subjects page does not state a CITI Human Subjects training requirement. Ask.

IACUC — animal subjects

The best-staffed compliance page on VSU’s site. Chair: Dr. Paul Kaseloo, 804-524-2996. Attending Veterinarian: Dr. Bom Harris. Administrative contact: Ms. Tracy Downey, 804-524-3083. Meeting dates are published for AY 2026–27.

confirm The OLAW Animal Welfare Assurance number is not published and PHS proposals need it. Request it from Dr. Kaseloo or Ms. Downey.

FCOI — the one that delays submissions

Governed by 42 CFR Part 50 Subpart F and NSF policy. Disclosure is required before you submit to any federal agency. VSU states plainly that non-compliance delays the submission. This is the single most avoidable reason a VSU proposal misses a deadline — file it now, not the week of.

Export control

Covers ITAR, EAR, and Treasury sanctions regulations, including “deemed exports” to foreign nationals on U.S. soil. Note that the fundamental research exclusion does not always cover equipment use. PIs must cooperate with OSRP before modifying project scope or staffing — adding an international collaborator mid-project is a compliance event, not just an admin one.

RCR training

CITI modules required for NSF, USDA, and PHS projects; register and affiliate with VSU at citiprogram.org. Monitored by OSRP. Do it before you need it.

Biosafety — an open question

confirm VSU’s compliance index lists Chemical Safety but no Institutional Biosafety Committee page and no biosafety officer appears on the public site. If your work involves recombinant DNA, select agents, or BSL-2 and above, raise this with DivRED before you propose it. Do not assume an IBC exists.

5. What you must confirm with a human

These are the questions to put in one email to OSRP the day you decide to apply. None of them have a reliable public answer.

  1. A copy of the signed F&A rate agreement PDF, for the proposal file. The rates themselves are confirmed at 44% on-campus / 20% off-campus MTDC.
  2. Is the 7-day Cayuse deadline calendar days or business days?
  3. Who currently signs as authorized official, and how should Dr. Faison’s title be rendered?
  4. Is there a limited-submission process? No policy, internal competition, or limited-submission list appears anywhere on vsu.edu. If a program restricts VSU to one nomination, someone has to be deciding — find out who.
  5. For a private foundation: which entity applies — the University, the VSU Foundation, or the VSU Research Foundation?
  6. Subaward and subrecipient monitoring process, and whether VSU participates in the Federal Demonstration Partnership. Not documented publicly.
  7. Effort certification cycle — semi-annual or quarterly.
  8. Current Research Integrity Officer, IRB chair, and OLAW assurance number.
  9. Whether an IBC exists, if your work needs one.
  10. For 1890/Evans-Allen work: does it route through REEport in addition to Cayuse?
