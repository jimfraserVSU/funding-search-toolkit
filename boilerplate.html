Boilerplate library

Write it once. Write it well. Stop rewriting it at midnight.

Every proposal needs a Facilities and Resources section, an institutional description, a data management plan, and letters that commit to something specific. Most people rebuild these under deadline, badly. Here they are, written properly, with the parts that must be checked marked in orange.

Read this before you copy anything. Every passage below contains [CONFIRM: …] markers. Those are places where a real number, name, or square footage belongs and I will not invent one. A proposal containing an invented facility, an outdated rate, or a fabricated enrollment figure is worse than one with a thinner narrative — reviewers check, and sponsored programs offices get audited.

Numbers come from the Office of Planning and Institutional Effectiveness (institutional data), VSU Research Statistics (expenditures, success rate), and OSRP (rates, identifiers). Request them early — they are not same-day.

2. Facilities, Equipment & Other Resources

NSF requires this as a separate document; NIH folds it into Factor 3 (Expertise and Resources), where it is judged “Appropriate” or “Additional resources needed.” The test reviewers apply is simple: could this project actually be done here? Generic descriptions of the campus fail that test. Named, specific resources pass it.

Facilities & Other Resources — general template
FACILITIES, EQUIPMENT, AND OTHER RESOURCES

Institutional Setting. Virginia State University is a public, four-year Historically Black
College and University and one of two land-grant institutions in the Commonwealth of Virginia.
In February 2025 VSU was designated a Carnegie R2 institution — High Research Spending and
Doctorate Production — one of fewer than fifteen HBCUs to hold that classification. The
University enrolls [CONFIRM: current enrollment — request from OPIE] students across six
degree-granting colleges and the College of Graduate Studies, and is located in Petersburg,
Virginia, within the Richmond–Petersburg metropolitan region.

Laboratory / Research Space. [CONFIRM: name the specific building, room numbers, and square
footage dedicated to this project. Reviewers discount unquantified space. Obtain from your
department chair or Facilities.]

Equipment. [CONFIRM: list only instruments you have verified are operational and available to
this project, with model and location. Do not list equipment that requires a reservation you
have not secured — describe the access arrangement instead.]

Computing. [CONFIRM: describe available computing with specifics — institutional cluster,
licensed software, storage allocation, and any external allocation such as ACCESS. If the
project needs computing VSU does not have, say so and budget for it; reviewers respect a
clear-eyed resource statement more than an optimistic one.]

Library and Information Resources. The Johnston Memorial Library provides access to
electronic databases, interlibrary loan, and research support services.
[CONFIRM: name the specific databases or collections this project depends on.]

Research Administration. The Division of Research and Economic Development houses the Office
of Sponsored Research and Programs, which provides pre-award development, budget preparation,
compliance oversight, and post-award financial administration. All proposals route through
Cayuse Sponsored Projects for institutional review and approval prior to submission. Research
Development provides funding identification, proposal development support, and grantsmanship
training.

Compliance Infrastructure. VSU maintains a registered Institutional Review Board
(FWA00027945) operating under 45 CFR 46, an Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee, and
programs in export control, financial conflict of interest (42 CFR Part 50 Subpart F), and
responsible conduct of research administered through CITI.
[CONFIRM with DivRED if this project involves recombinant DNA, select agents, or BSL-2+ work —
no Institutional Biosafety Committee is documented publicly.]

Institutional Data. The Office of Planning and Institutional Effectiveness maintains
institutional research capacity including the Common Data Set and SCHEV peer comparisons,
supporting evaluation, benchmarking, and reporting requirements.

Student Research Infrastructure. The Trojan Center for Undergraduate Applied Research and the
VSU Graduate Research Initiative support mentored student research across disciplines,
providing a structured pathway for the student participation described in this proposal.

3. Institutional description — three lengths

Foundations want two sentences. Federal narratives want a paragraph. Capacity proposals want the full case. Same facts, three depths.

Short — 2 sentences, for foundation LOIs and cover letters
Virginia State University is a public Historically Black College and University and one of two
land-grant institutions in the Commonwealth of Virginia, designated a Carnegie R2 institution
for high research spending and doctorate production in 2025. VSU serves a predominantly
first-generation and historically underserved student population across six colleges in the
Richmond–Petersburg region.
Medium — one paragraph, for federal narratives
Virginia State University is a public, four-year Historically Black College and University
founded in 1882 and located in Petersburg, Virginia. VSU is one of two land-grant institutions
in the Commonwealth and holds 1890 land-grant designation, carrying a research and extension
mission focused on small, part-time, and limited-resource producers. In February 2025 the
University was designated Carnegie R2 — High Research Spending and Doctorate Production —
placing it among fewer than fifteen HBCUs nationally at that classification. VSU enrolls
[CONFIRM: enrollment — OPIE] students across six degree-granting colleges and the College of
Graduate Studies, and is a federally recognized Minority-Serving Institution. Its position in
the Richmond–Petersburg corridor gives the University sustained access to communities, school
systems, and producers that are central to [CONFIRM: name the specific access this project
depends on — do not claim access you have not arranged].
Long — for capacity and infrastructure proposals
INSTITUTIONAL CONTEXT AND RESEARCH CAPACITY

Virginia State University is a public, four-year Historically Black College and University
founded in 1882, one of two land-grant institutions in the Commonwealth of Virginia, and a
federally recognized Minority-Serving Institution. VSU holds 1890 land-grant designation and
operates an Agricultural Research Station and Cooperative Extension program serving small,
part-time, and limited-resource producers across the Commonwealth.

In February 2025, VSU was designated a Carnegie R2 institution — High Research Spending and
Doctorate Production. This classification reflects sustained growth in externally funded
research and in doctoral production, and situates the University among fewer than fifteen HBCUs
nationally at that level. In 2026 VSU became a founding member of a national HBCU coalition
advancing toward top research status.

Current research capacity. [CONFIRM: annual research expenditures, number of active external
awards, grant success rate, and doctoral degrees conferred — all available from VSU Research
Statistics and OPIE. These numbers are the backbone of this section; a capacity narrative
without them reads as aspiration rather than evidence.]

Research administration. The Division of Research and Economic Development consolidates the
Office of Sponsored Research and Programs, Research Compliance, Research Development, the
Trojan Center for Undergraduate Applied Research, and the VSU Graduate Research Initiative
under unified leadership. All proposals route through Cayuse Sponsored Projects, and the
University operates a structured pre-proposal planning process that convenes Procurement,
Information Technology, Human Resources, and Facilities alongside the investigator before
submission — an unusually deliberate coordination mechanism for an institution of this size.

Institutional position and access. VSU's location and mission provide access that is difficult
to replicate at larger research institutions: [CONFIRM: name the specific communities, school
divisions, producer networks, clinical partners, or agencies this project will work with, and
the nature of the existing relationship. Access asserted is worth nothing; access documented
with a letter is worth a great deal.]

What this award would build. [CONFIRM: state the durable institutional capability that will
exist at the end of this award and does not exist today — a core facility, a functioning
process, a trained cohort, a sustained partnership, a data infrastructure — and who sustains
it after the award period. Reviewers of capacity programs read this paragraph first.]

4. The HBCU / 1890 / R2 capacity paragraph

The most common mistake in this section is assertion. Being an HBCU is not an argument. Access is an argument. What does this institution reach — which students, which producers, which communities, which regional problems — that a large research university does not? Name it, quantify it where you can, and document it with a letter. Reviewers have read a thousand paragraphs asserting institutional identity and very few demonstrating institutional leverage.
Capacity and leverage — template
INSTITUTIONAL CAPACITY AND LEVERAGE

The capability gap. At present, VSU [CONFIRM: state honestly what the institution cannot
currently do that this award would enable — specific instrumentation, methodological expertise,
data infrastructure, or research administration capacity. A candid baseline makes the rest of
this section credible; an inflated one makes reviewers distrust everything above it.]

What this award builds. By the end of the project period, VSU will have [CONFIRM: the specific
durable asset — a functioning core facility, a trained faculty cohort, an established
partnership with a named entity, a maintained dataset]. This capability will persist beyond the
award because [CONFIRM: the actual sustaining mechanism — an institutional line, a fee-for-
service model, an established course, a renewed partnership agreement. "We will seek additional
funding" reads to reviewers as "this ends when the money ends."]

Institutional leverage. VSU's position provides access that this project requires and that is
not readily available elsewhere: [CONFIRM: the specific access. Examples of the right level of
specificity — a named school division serving a defined student population; an extension
network reaching a documented number of limited-resource producers; a longstanding relationship
with a named community organization; a student body whose composition makes a research question
answerable that could not be answered on a different campus.]

Institutional commitment. [CONFIRM: what the University is actually committing — space,
release time, a matching contribution, a personnel line, a policy change — and at what level of
authority it has been approved. Route through OSRP; voluntary committed cost share becomes a
binding, auditable obligation.]

Evaluation. Progress toward these capacity outcomes will be assessed through [CONFIRM: what is
measured, by whom, against what baseline, at what interval, and what happens if a target is
missed. Capacity programs expect an evaluation plan; most applications supply activities
instead of measures.]

5. Broader Impacts — institutional context

NSF evaluates Broader Impacts against all five review elements, including whether the plan “incorporates a mechanism to assess success.” A list of activities with no evaluation, no named person, and no budget line is the single most reliably criticized weakness in NSF panels.

Broader Impacts — institutional context opening
BROADER IMPACTS

Context. Virginia State University is a Historically Black College and University and a
Minority-Serving Institution enrolling a predominantly [CONFIRM: describe the student
population accurately using OPIE data — first-generation share, Pell-eligible share,
demographic composition. Use real figures.] student body. Research conducted here reaches
students who remain substantially underrepresented in [CONFIRM: the specific field], and the
mentored research experiences embedded in this project are therefore not incidental to its
broader impacts — they are a primary mechanism of them.

Activities and outcomes.
  Activity: [CONFIRM: what specifically happens]
  Participants: [CONFIRM: how many, recruited how, from where]
  Intended outcome: [CONFIRM: the change you expect, stated so it could be disproven]
  Measure: [CONFIRM: what is measured, by whom, when, compared to what]
  Responsible person: [CONFIRM: a named individual with allocated effort — not "the PI, in
    addition to everything else"]
  Budget line: [CONFIRM: point to it. Unbudgeted broader impacts read as unserious.]

Assessment. [CONFIRM: describe the evaluation design — pre/post measures, comparison group
where feasible, external evaluator where the scale warrants it, and how findings will adjust
the activity mid-course.]

Dissemination. Findings and materials will be shared through [CONFIRM: specific venues,
repositories, and partner channels — and note that NSF now requires deposit of the Author
Accepted Manuscript at publication with no embargo period].

6. Data Management & Sharing Plan

NSF changed this recently. Under PAPPG 24-1 Supplement 2 (effective January 22, 2026), the 12-month public access embargo is removed, Author Accepted Manuscript deposit is required at publication, and Data Management and Sharing Plans moved to a dedicated Research.gov tool as of April 27, 2026. Sharing must now be addressed “as an integral part of review.” NIH has its own DMS Policy requirements. Check the current announcement — this is the fastest-moving requirement in federal proposals.
Data Management & Sharing — skeleton
DATA MANAGEMENT AND SHARING PLAN

1. Data types and volume. This project will generate [CONFIRM: describe each data type —
instrument output, survey responses, interview transcripts, administrative records, imagery,
code — with estimated volume and file formats].

2. Related tools, software, and code. [CONFIRM: name analysis software and any code that will
be produced; state the license under which code will be released.]

3. Standards. Data will be documented using [CONFIRM: the metadata standard appropriate to
the field]. Variables, instruments, and collection procedures will be documented in a codebook
maintained alongside the data.

4. Preservation and access. Data will be deposited in [CONFIRM: name a specific repository —
ICPSR, Dryad, a domain repository, or an institutional option. "An appropriate repository" is
not an answer]. Data will be available beginning [CONFIRM: timing] and retained for
[CONFIRM: period].

5. Access, distribution, and reuse. Data will be released under [CONFIRM: license]. Access
will be [open / controlled], because [CONFIRM: reason].

6. Protections and limitations. Human subjects data will be de-identified prior to deposit in
accordance with the protocol approved by the VSU Institutional Review Board (FWA00027945).
[CONFIRM: describe any restriction arising from participant consent, tribal or community data
governance, proprietary agreements, or export control. State plainly what cannot be shared and
why — reviewers accept justified limits and penalize vague ones.]

7. Oversight. [CONFIRM: the named individual responsible for data management, with allocated
effort, and the institutional support available to them.]

7. Mentoring plan

Mentoring plan — students and postdocs
MENTORING PLAN

Structure. Each [undergraduate researcher / graduate student / postdoctoral scholar] supported
by this project will work under a written Individual Development Plan reviewed at the start of
the appointment and revisited [CONFIRM: interval]. The plan will cover research skills, career
goals, and a timeline of concrete milestones.

Research training. Mentees will [CONFIRM: state what they will actually DO — not "assist with
research." Name the techniques, analyses, and independent responsibilities they will hold, and
the trajectory from supervised to independent work.]

Professional development. Support will include responsible conduct of research training through
CITI, [CONFIRM: conference presentation with named target venue and budgeted travel; manuscript
authorship expectations stated explicitly; grant-writing exposure; networking with the project's
partner organizations].

Institutional supports. Mentees will have access to the Trojan Center for Undergraduate Applied
Research or the VSU Graduate Research Initiative as appropriate to their level, providing
structured research training beyond the immediate project team.

Contact and assessment. The PI will meet with each mentee [CONFIRM: frequency] individually and
[CONFIRM: frequency] as a group. Progress against the Individual Development Plan will be
assessed [CONFIRM: interval], and outcomes tracked including [CONFIRM: presentations,
publications, degree completion, and placement into graduate programs or positions].

8. Letters of commitment

A generic enthusiasm letter is worse than no letter. It tells a reviewer the partner has agreed to nothing. Draft the specific commitment yourself, send it to the partner to edit and put on their letterhead, and make sure the narrative promises exactly what the letters commit to — reviewers compare them.

do not overstate it].

  - [CONFIRM: access — e.g., "recruitment support for up to 120 participants through our

[ORGANIZATION] beyond what is described above.

A working habit. Keep one file with your own verified versions of these sections, dated. Re-verify the table above once a year and after any leadership change. The point of boilerplate is not to stop thinking — it is to stop re-deciding things you already decided well, so your attention goes to the science.
