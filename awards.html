Award intel

Stop guessing what a program funds. Go read what it funded.

Announcements describe what a program says it wants. The award record shows what it actually bought — at what size, to what kind of institution, for what kind of project. This is the most underused move in grant seeking, and both databases are free.

How to use this well. Before you write a word, pull ten to fifteen funded projects in your target program. Notice the scale of the awards, how the titles are framed, whether they are single-PI or team, and which institutions keep appearing. You are calibrating against reality instead of against the announcement's aspirations. Then look at the peers tab: if institutions like yours are winning, the question stops being “can we?” and becomes “what did they have that we need?”

Why this matters beyond curiosity. Capacity narratives, institutional-commitment sections, and “prior support” statements all need real numbers, and reviewers distrust narratives without them. This is also the fastest way to find an internal collaborator who has already won from the agency you are targeting.
