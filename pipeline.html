My pipeline

Searching is the easy part. Remembering what you already started is the hard part.

Stars mark what looked interesting. This tracks what you actually did about it — what you are drafting, what went out the door and when, and what came back. It also gives you the one thing every annual review asks for and nobody keeps: a dated list of what you submitted.

Read this before you rely on it. This list lives in this browser on this computer and nowhere else. It is not an account, there is no server copy, and clearing your browsing data will erase it. That is a deliberate trade — the site has no logins and collects nothing about you — but it means the backup below is not optional housekeeping. Export a backup whenever you add something you would hate to retype. The file restores in one click, on any machine.

Add an opportunity

How this connects to the rest of the site. Star anything on the Search or Funders pages, then use Import my starred list here to pull those entries in with their sponsor, deadline and link already filled. From there they are yours to annotate — the pipeline never changes on its own, so nothing you have written gets overwritten by a later import.

Two ways to get these into your calendar, and they are not the same. Add deadlines to my calendar downloads a one-time .ics file covering every tracked item that has a date, including ones you typed in yourself, each with a 30-day and 7-day reminder. It is a snapshot — change a date here afterwards and your calendar will not know. Subscribe to tracked deadlines gives you a live feed URL that keeps itself current, but it can only carry items that came from the programme directory: manually added entries live only in your browser, and the server that builds the feed has never seen them. The download covers everything; the subscription stays current. Most people want both.

The annual review use. Export CSV gives you one row per opportunity with dates, amounts, your role and outcome — the format that drops straight into an annual report, a promotion dossier, or a chair's request for departmental grant activity. It is easier to keep this current in ninety seconds a month than to reconstruct a year of it from memory each spring.
